Who I Am

A few years ahead of your bochurim, on the same derech.

I spent Shana Alef at Yishrei Lev in Eretz Yisroel, and two years in Rabbi Barry Klein's chabura. In between, I spent a year as a dorm counselor at Waterbury Mesivta, where I gave a chabura on Pirkei Avos and trained at Nova União. I know what the year in Israel is. I know yeshiva life. I know what dorm energy on a Motzei Shabbos looks like.

I've trained in martial arts since I was 14: Krav Maga, Muay Thai, MMA, and Brazilian Jiu Jitsu. Today I train with ATOS Jerusalem and the Israeli national team.

I'm not an onlooker. I'm a ben Torah who happens to compete at the highest level. I built this program for the guy I was: the one who needed this outlet and didn't have it.

— Zev

Why It Works

Mussar on the mat.

Bochurim carry real energy and real stress. Jiu Jitsu channels both into discipline, humility, and physical confidence in a structured and supervised setting inside your own walls. You tap, you learn, you get back up. No co-ed gym. No outside environment. No travel.

How It Works

You bring the space. That's it.

A park down the block, a shiur room that's free for the hour, a dining room after seder. If there's open floor, it works. I bring the mats and all the gear.

Classes run 3–6 bochurim, so everyone gets real attention.

Safety

Structured. Supervised. Injury-conscious.

Every class opens with a full warm-up and runs technique-first. Beginners drill controlled positions long before anything live, and all sparring is supervised and matched by size and experience.
